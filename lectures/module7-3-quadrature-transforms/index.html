    if isnothing(k)

1. Recap on Gauss Quadrature¶

Newton–Cotes quadrature corresponds to equispaced points, Clenshaw–Curtis quadrature to Chebyshev points, and Gauss quadrature to Legendre points.¶

We want to approximate

∫−11f(x)dx≈∑i=1nwif(xi)=wTf(x)\int_{-1}^1 f(x) dx \approx \sum_{i=1}^n w_i f(x_i) = \mathbf w^T f(\mathbf x)∫−11​f(x)dx≈i=1∑n​wi​f(xi​)=wTf(x)
(1)

We call wiw_iwi​ the quadrature weights and xix_ixi​ the quadrature points or abscissa.

How do we choose the points?

Recall that the Legendre polynomials P0(x)=1P_0(x) = 1P0​(x)=1, P1(x)=xP_1(x) = xP1​(x)=x, ..., are pairwise orthogonal

∫−11Pm(x)Pn(x)=0,∀m≠n.\int_{-1}^1 P_m(x) P_n(x) = 0, \quad \forall m\ne n.∫−11​Pm​(x)Pn​(x)=0,∀m=n.
(2)

How does this work?¶

Suppose a polynomial P(x)P(x)P(x) on the interval [−1,1][-1,1][−1,1] can be written as

P(x)=Pn(x)q(x)+r(x)P(x) = P_n(x) q(x) + r(x)P(x)=Pn​(x)q(x)+r(x)
(3)

where Pn(x)P_n(x)Pn​(x) is the nnn th Legendre polnomials and both q(x)q(x)q(x) and r(x)r(x)r(x) are polynomials of maximum degree n−1n-1n−1. We know we can write this form by using long division of polynomials.

  • Why is ∫−11Pn(x)q(x)=0\int_{-1}^1 P_n(x) q(x) = 0∫−11​Pn​(x)q(x)=0?

    • Since Gaussian quadrature is exact on the polynomial r(x)r(x)r(x), since it is just integration of the interpolating polynomial of degree n−1n-1n−1, which is identical to r(x)r(x)r(x).

  • Can every polynomials of degree 2n−12n-12n−1 be written in the above form?

    • Yes, by long division of polynomials.

  • How many roots does Pn(x)P_n(x)Pn​(x) have on the interval?

    • nnn complex-valued, considering their multiplicity (Fundamental Theorem of Algebra: every non-zero, single-variable, degree nnn polynomial with complex coefficients has, counted with multiplicity, exactly nnn complex roots.).

  • Can we choose points {xi}\{x_i\}{xi​} such that the first term is 0?

    • At the roots xix_ixi​ of the nnn-th Legendre polynomial, P(xi)=r(xi)P(x_i) = r(x_i)P(xi​)=r(xi​), since Pn(xi)=0P_n (x_i ) = 0Pn​(xi​)=0 for all iii.

If Pn(xi)=0P_n(x_i) = 0Pn​(xi​)=0 for each xix_ixi​, then we need only integrate r(x)r(x)r(x), which is done exactly by integrating its interpolating polynomial. How do we find these roots xix_ixi​?

We approximate

∫−1−1f(x)dx≈∑i=1nwif(xi)\int_{-1}^{-1} f(x) dx \approx \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i f(x_i)∫−1−1​f(x)dx≈i=1∑n​wi​f(xi​)
(4)

where wiw_iwi​ are the weights defined as

wi=∫−11Li(x)dxi=1,…,nw_i = \int_{-1}^{1}L_i(x) dx \quad i=1, \dots, nwi​=∫−11​Li​(x)dxi=1,…,n
(5)

the Lagrange interpolating polynomial with nnn interpolating nodes, and the quadrature nodes xix_ixi​ are the roots of the nnn-th Legendre polynomial on [−1,1][-1,1][−1,1].

Note:

To approximate integrals on a general interval [a,b][a,b][a,b], the problem needs to be translated to [−1,1][-1,1][−1,1] with a simple change of variables. Using the substitution t=(2x−a−b)/(b−a)t = (2x - a - b) / (b-a)t=(2x−a−b)/(b−a), we find

∫abf(x)dx=∫−11f((b−a)t+b+a2)(b−a)2dt\int_a^b f(x) dx = \int_{-1}^{1} f \left( \frac{(b-a)t + b + a}{2} \right) \frac{(b-a)}{2} dt∫ab​f(x)dx=∫−11​f(2(b−a)t+b+a​)2(b−a)​dt
(6)

Spectral Element Method (SEM)¶

Gauss-Lobatto nodes are preferred in the quadrature formulae for the Spectral Element Method (SEM)--a high-order version of the Finite Element Method (FEM) defined on non-uniform points--, which achieves “spectral” or exponential" convergence for the solution of Partial Differential Equations.

In finite element formulations, the weak form of a Partial Differential Equation (PDE) is evaluated on a subdomain Ωe\Omega_eΩe​ (element) and the local results are composed into a larger system of equations that models the entire problem on the global domain Ω\OmegaΩ. The weak-form involves the L2L^2L2 inner product (integral) of a variational formulation with suitable basis functions and trial/test functions.

Gauss-Lobatto nodes are preferred because they include nodes at the endpoints -1 and 1, hence ensuring continuity of the basis functions across element boundaries.

The more general formulation of SEM, that instead of special points, uses equispaced points is called the Finite Element Method (FEM).

Finite Element Methods (FEM)¶

Main ideas:

  • Need to integrate the product of basis functions over every element

  • Choose a quadrature (e.g., Gauss) to minimize number of points for sufficient accuracy

  • Bad effects if insufficient points

Tanh-Sinh quadrature¶

Main idea: make everything smooth.

When functions have singularities near the endpoints, it is usually more efficient to integrate via a change of variables. Suppose we have a strictly monotone differentiable function ϕ:(−∞,∞)→(−1,1)\phi: (-\infty, \infty) \to (-1, 1)ϕ:(−∞,∞)→(−1,1).

Then with x=ϕ(s)x = \phi(s)x=ϕ(s), our integral transforms as

∫−11f(x)dx=∫−∞∞f(ϕ(s))ϕ′(s)ds.\int_{-1}^1 f(x) \mathrm dx = \int_{-\infty}^\infty f(\phi(s)) \phi'(s) \mathrm d s .∫−11​f(x)dx=∫−∞∞​f(ϕ(s))ϕ′(s)ds.
(7)

The tanh-sinh method uses a transformation such that ϕ′(s)→0\phi'(s) \to 0ϕ′(s)→0 faster than the singularity f(ϕ(s))f(\phi(s))f(ϕ(s)) grows, such that the integrand goes to 0 at finite sss.

tanhsinh(s) = tanh(pi/2*sinh(s))

function dtanhsinh(s)
    ds = 1
    t = pi/2 * sinh(s)
    dt = pi/2 * cosh(s) * ds
    (1 - tanh(t)^2) * dt
end
