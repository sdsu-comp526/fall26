1. Matrices as linear transformations¶

Tip

Resource: An excellent resource for Linear Algebra is the MIT OpenCourseWare course by Prof. Gilbert Strang (now retired). Here is a link to the Fall 2011 undergraduate course edition. Other video galleries of his lectures can also be found.

Linear algebra is the study of linear transformations on vectors, which represent points in a finite dimensional space. The matrix-vector product y=Axy = A xy=Ax is a linear combination of the columns of AAA. The familiar definition,

yi=∑jAi,jxjy_i = \sum_j A_{i,j} x_jyi​=j∑​Ai,j​xj​
(1)

can also be viewed as

y=[A:,1∣A:,2∣⋯][x1x2⋮]=[A:,1]x1+[A:,2]x2+⋯ .y = \Bigg[ A_{:,1} \Bigg| A_{:,2} \Bigg| \dotsm \Bigg] \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ \vdots \end{bmatrix} = \Bigg[ A_{:,1} \Bigg] x_1 + \Bigg[ A_{:,2} \Bigg] x_2 + \dotsb .y=[A:,1​∣∣​A:,2​∣∣​⋯]⎣⎡​x1​x2​⋮​⎦⎤​=[A:,1​]x1​+[A:,2​]x2​+⋯.
(2)

That is, a linear combination of the columns of A.

Math and Julia Notation¶

The notation Ai,jA_{i,j}Ai,j​ corresponds to the Julia syntax A[i,j] and the colon : means the entire range (row or column). So A:,jA_{:,j}A:,j​ is the jjjth column and Ai,:A_{i,:}Ai,:​ is the iiith row. The corresponding Julia syntax is A[:,j] and A[i,:].

Implementing multiplication by row¶

function matmult1(A, x)
    m, n = size(A)
    y = zeros(m)
    for i in 1:m # row index first, i.e., for each row
        for j in 1:n # we iterate over the columns
            y[i] += A[i,j] * x[j] # we apply the familiar definition
        end
    end
    y
end

function matmult2(A, x)
    m, n = size(A)
    y = zeros(m)
    for i in 1:m # iterate over rows of A
        y[i] = A[i,:]' * x # this way we use the dot product between the transposed row of A and the whole x
    end
    y
end

Implementing multiplication by column¶

function matmult3(A, x)
    m, n = size(A)
    y = zeros(m)
    for j in 1:n # iterate over columns of A
        y += A[:, j] * x[j]
    end
    y
end

2. Polynomial evaluation¶

Polynomial evaluation is (continuous) linear algebra¶

Vandermonde matrices¶

A Vandermonde matrix is one whose columns are polynomials (monomials) evaluated at discrete points.

V(x)=[1∣x∣x2∣x3∣⋯]V(x) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \Bigg| x \Bigg| x^2 \Bigg| x^3 \Bigg| \dotsb \end{bmatrix}V(x)=[1∣∣​x∣∣​x2∣∣​x3∣∣​⋯​]
(4)
function vander(x, k=nothing)
    if isnothing(k)
        k = length(x)
    end
    m = length(x)
    V = ones(m, k)
    for j in 2:k
        V[:, j] = V[:, j-1] .* x
    end
    V
end

Some common terminology¶

  • The range of AAA is the space spanned by its columns. This definition coincides with the range of a function f(x)f(x)f(x) when f(x)=Axf(x) = A xf(x)=Ax.

  • The (right) nullspace of AAA is the space of vectors xxx such that Ax=0A x = 0Ax=0.

  • The rank of AAA is the dimension of its range.

  • A matrix has full rank if the nullspace of either AAA or ATA^TAT is empty (only the 0 vector). Equivalently, if all the columns of AAA (or ATA^TAT) are linearly independent.

  • A nonsingular (or invertible) matrix is a square matrix of full rank. We call the inverse A−1A^{-1}A−1 and it satisfies A−1A=AA−1=IA^{-1} A = A A^{-1} = IA−1A=AA−1=I.

  1. AAA has an inverse, A−1A^{-1}A−1

  2. rank⁡(A)=m\operatorname{rank} (A) = mrank(A)=m

  5. det⁡(A)≠0\det(A) \ne 0det(A)=0

What is an inverse?¶

When we write x=A−1yx = A^{-1} yx=A−1y, we mean that xxx is the unique vector such that Ax=yA x = yAx=y. (It is rare that we explicitly compute a matrix A−1A^{-1}A−1, though it’s not as “bad” as people may have told you.) A vector yyy is equivalent to ∑ieiyi\sum_i e_i y_i∑i​ei​yi​ where eie_iei​ are columns of the identity. Meanwhile, x=A−1yx = A^{-1} yx=A−1y means that we are expressing that same vector yyy in the basis of the columns of AAA, i.e., ∑iA:,ixi\sum_i A_{:,i} x_i∑i​A:,i​xi​.

3. Orthogonality¶

The inner product

xTy=∑ixiyix^T y = \sum_i x_i y_ixTy=i∑​xi​yi​
(6)

of real vectors (or columns of a matrix) tells us about their magnitude and about the angle. The norm is induced by the inner product,

∥x∥=xTx\lVert x \rVert = \sqrt{x^T x}∥x∥=xTx​
(7)

and the angle θ\thetaθ is defined by

cos⁡θ=xTy∥x∥ ∥y∥.\cos \theta = \frac{x^T y}{\lVert x \rVert \, \lVert y \rVert} .cosθ=∥x∥∥y∥xTy​.
(8)

Inner products are bilinear, which means that they satisfy some convenient algebraic properties

(x+y)Tz=xTz+yTzxT(y+z)=xTy+xTz(αx)T(βy)=αβxTy.\begin{split} (x + y)^T z &= x^T z + y^T z \\ x^T (y + z) &= x^T y + x^T z \\ (\alpha x)^T (\beta y) &= \alpha \beta x^T y \\ \end{split} .(x+y)TzxT(y+z)(αx)T(βy)​=xTz+yTz=xTy+xTz=αβxTy​.
(9)
